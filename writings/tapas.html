Utkantha and Tapas(curiosity and action)

"Utkantha" means having curiosity for seeking truth, which comes with intense hard work called "Tapas." In my process of learning and creating things, I am coming across so many things that are making me more curious and proud on humans.

The scene from Space Odyssey (2001) is profoundly moving. In the opening of the movie, chimps are hunting and gathering; later on, by coincidence, a chimp took the bone in his hand and figured out that he could use that as a tool for his survival. When he threw that bone in the air, it became a space station. This reminds me of the progress we made through asking questions, being ambitious, and becoming curious about nature and the universe.

We have always been curious about the things around us; we can see from the earliest time that people wanted to discover the answers to some of the important questions about life and the meaning of all of this. The sages of the Upanishads also had this Utkantha about the invariant of experience and matter. Greeks and Romans were curious about this kind of question too. Lucretius expressed this curiosity in De rerum natura (On the Nature of Things).

One of the biggest discoveries of humans is 'science,' which can create new knowledge and help us understand the fabric of reality. I remember when David Deutsch said that a small sheet is adequate for writing all the important foundational equations of physics. To put this into perspective, all the laws that you wrote on the sheet explain vast physical reality. You can write 249 characters on a human hair or 1,749 characters on one grain of rice; the equation of relativity can be written on both rice and hair. Imagine the scale of the Earth, the Sun, and the gravitational space between them, and why just those? Imagine the physical scale of gravitational space in the universe; all this is compressed into only a few symbols. These symbols are math. We have the language of math, which helps us articulate physical reality in an elegant way.

For a long time in history, we did not have an explanation when someone asked about the origin of human beings, and then we figured out that there is an algorithm called Evolution running for millions of years, and it is the answer to our origin. We remained in discomfort about not knowing the answer to some questions for a long time, but that paid off.

The fact that I can read about Lucretius, the Upanishads, and all these works of science itself shows our innovation of writing, which helps to democratize knowledge and information all around the world. Imagine all this written curiosity will remain with its creator and not with other peoples. Imagine Newton discovered gravity remains with Newton itself.

We have internet, which made this information accessible at your click. This is crazy to think that someone wrote a book thousands of years ago and I typed the name of the book and then read that book. The internet is one of those innovation that can represent us among extraterrestrial civilizations.

We learned to turn electricity into computation. As Steve Jobs said, "Humanity split before computers and after computers." The transistor, which turns electricity into computation through bits, is 1 cm big. When Bell Labs innovated, now comparing that to the most advanced chip, it has 336 billion transistors, where each transistor has become so small that in one human hair we can fit 20,000 to 25,000 modern transistors.

Our curiosity now moved to innovate intelligence and understand the human mind. With deep learning we are training larger and larger neural networks; for example, GPT-2 has 1.5 billion parameters, and we now have models with 1 trillion parameters for approximation. The human mind has 100T-500T connections. hardware for training this neural network like modern Nvidia GPU can do 4 quadrillion (4,000 trillion) calculations per second.

With this model, anybody can open their device and ask questions; their curiosity will be satisfied. A chimp figured out that a bone can be used as a tool. What if he sees this neural network? How does he feel? Imagine somebody even just 200 years ago saw this thing; they'd be so surprised to see it. that you can talk to your computer. A few years ago, if I were staring at stars and getting some questions, then I would not have had that technology where I could instantly ask questions to it and get answers from it.


All because of Utkantha and the pursuit of truth.
